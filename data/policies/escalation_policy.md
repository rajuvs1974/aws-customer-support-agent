# Customer Support Escalation Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines situations that require customer support cases to be escalated to specialized teams or management.

## General Escalation Principle

Customer support should attempt to resolve routine customer requests using approved policies and available systems.

Cases should be escalated when the issue:

- Requires authorization beyond the support representative's role.
- Involves conflicting information.
- Requires investigation by another team.
- Presents significant financial or customer impact.
- Involves suspected fraud or security concerns.

## Shipment Escalation

Shipment-related cases should be escalated when:

- A shipment is delayed by more than 5 business days.
- A carrier confirms that a shipment is lost.
- Tracking information is inconsistent with the order record.
- A high-value shipment is missing.
- The customer disputes the carrier's status.
- A carrier investigation has been unsuccessful.

The escalation should include the order ID, shipment ID, customer ID, current tracking status, promised delivery date, and relevant case history.

## Refund Escalation

Refund requests should be escalated when:

- The requested refund exceeds the support authorization limit.
- The customer disputes a previous refund decision.
- Payment information conflicts with the order information.
- A refund has already been issued for the same order.
- The customer requests an exception to the standard refund policy.

## Cancellation Escalation

Cancellation cases should be escalated when:

- The order has already entered fulfillment.
- The order has already shipped.
- The customer disputes a cancellation fee.
- The order status is inconsistent across systems.
- The customer requests an exception to the normal cancellation rules.

## Damaged Package Escalation

Damaged-package cases should be escalated when:

- The product is high value.
- The customer disputes the damage assessment.
- Required evidence is unavailable but the customer disputes the decision.
- A replacement has already been issued.
- A refund has already been issued.
- Carrier responsibility is unclear.

## Customer Complaint Escalation

Customer complaints should be escalated when:

- The customer requests management involvement.
- The customer reports repeated unresolved service failures.
- The customer threatens legal or regulatory action.
- The issue involves significant financial impact.
- The issue involves potential discrimination, harassment, or safety concerns.

## Fraud and Security

Cases involving suspected fraud, account compromise, unauthorized transactions, or security incidents should be escalated to the appropriate security or fraud team.

Customer support representatives should not attempt to independently investigate security incidents beyond their authorized procedures.

## Priority Levels

### Priority 1 — Critical

Immediate escalation is required for issues involving:

- Customer safety
- Security incidents
- Account compromise
- Significant financial risk

### Priority 2 — High

Escalate as soon as possible for:

- High-value lost shipments
- Major service failures
- Significant customer impact
- Repeated unresolved incidents

### Priority 3 — Standard

Escalate through the normal workflow for:

- Policy exceptions
- Routine disputes
- Cases requiring another team's investigation

## Escalation Information

Every escalation should contain enough information for the receiving team to investigate without repeatedly asking the customer for the same information.

Include:

- Customer ID
- Order ID
- Shipment ID when applicable
- Case ID
- Issue summary
- Relevant dates
- Current status
- Actions already taken
- Requested resolution
- Supporting evidence when applicable

## Customer Communication

When a case is escalated, the customer should be told:

- That the case requires additional review.
- Which team is reviewing the issue when appropriate.
- What information has been submitted.
- What the next expected step is.

Support representatives should not promise an outcome or resolution time unless an approved service commitment exists.
